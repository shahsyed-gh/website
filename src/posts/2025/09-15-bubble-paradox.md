# Building Real Value in an Inflated Market

The AI industry presents product managers with a striking contradiction: we're simultaneously in a bubble where "someone is going to lose a phenomenal amount of money," as OpenAI CEO Sam Altman warns, while companies like Sierra achieve $10 billion valuations by fundamentally transforming enterprise operations. This paradox mirrors the dot-com era perfectly. Webvan collapsed attempting grocery delivery in 1999, yet Instacart thrives today solving the same problem. The difference wasn't the idea but the timing, infrastructure maturity, and most critically, the focus on sustainable business models over technology evangelism. For product managers, this historical parallel offers a clear lesson: bubbles burst around companies selling promises, not those delivering measurable outcomes.

Sierra's approach demonstrates how to build through the hype cycle. By charging only when their AI agents successfully resolve customer issues, they've aligned their economics with actual value creation rather than potential capabilities. This outcome-based pricing model forces product discipline that speculation-funded competitors lack. When Bret Taylor notes that voice interactions already exceed text on their platform, it reveals something profound about market adoption: customers don't care about AI, they care about solving the expensive problem of phone support that costs $10-20 per interaction. Product managers should internalize this distinction between AI tourism (performative projects that showcase technology) and AI transformation (solutions that fundamentally alter unit economics). The winners emerging from this bubble won't be those with the most sophisticated models, but those who packaged AI's capabilities into solutions for specific, painful business problems with clear ROI metrics.
