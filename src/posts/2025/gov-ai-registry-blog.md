# Public Registries: The Missing Feature in Enterprise AI Products

Canada's federal government is pioneering a public registry to track and disclose its AI initiatives across all departments, addressing a critical gap that many enterprise products overlook: transparent AI governance. The initiative emerged from the government's realization that departments were independently experimenting with AI tools, from Fisheries and Oceans using AI to locate lost fishing gear to Transport Canada screening high-risk cargo. This fragmented approach led to redundancy and lack of oversight, prompting the creation of a centralized AI strategy with public accountability at its core.

For product managers building enterprise solutions, this government approach reveals an untapped market opportunity. As organizations rapidly adopt AI tools, they face similar challenges: shadow AI deployments, redundant investments, and growing demands for transparency from stakeholders. Products that offer AI discovery, cataloging, and reporting features could fill this void. Consider how a SaaS platform might automatically detect AI usage across an organization, generate compliance reports, and provide dashboards for both internal governance teams and external stakeholders. The government's dual-purpose registry design, serving both public transparency and internal coordination needs, suggests that successful products should balance multiple user personas. This approach transforms AI governance from a compliance burden into a strategic advantage, particularly as regulations like the EU AI Act demand greater accountability.
