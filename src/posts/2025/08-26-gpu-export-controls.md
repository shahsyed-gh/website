# Supply Chain Vulnerability in the Age of Export Controls

The emergence of a billion-dollar GPU black market following US export restrictions offers critical lessons for product managers navigating geopolitical supply chain risks. GamersNexus's investigation reveals how NVIDIA GPUs, banned from direct export to China, continue flowing through Singapore middlemen, student smugglers, and modified consumer hardware at premium prices. For product teams in hardware companies, this demonstrates the futility of single-point regulatory controls when demand exceeds policy constraints. The black market's sophistication, from salvaged RTX 5090 chips to memory-expanded RTX 4090s, shows how resourceful actors will modify existing products to meet market needs that official channels cannot serve.

Product managers must recognize that export controls create both risks and opportunities in global hardware markets. NVIDIA's revenue shift to Singapore (18% of total revenue) while actual shipments remain under 2% illustrates how companies adapt billing structures to navigate restrictions without violating compliance requirements. The "close one eye, open one eye" approach described in the investigation suggests that companies may benefit from regulatory ambiguity while maintaining plausible deniability. For product strategy, this highlights the importance of building flexible distribution networks, understanding regulatory arbitrage opportunities, and preparing for scenarios where official market access disappears overnight. Smart product teams should develop contingency plans that account for both compliance requirements and the reality that determined customers will find alternative procurement methods.
