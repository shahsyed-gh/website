# The traditional AI conference model—centralized, in-person mega-events—is buckling under explosive growth. 

From skyrocketing paper submissions and overwhelmed peer review systems to massive travel-related carbon footprints and rising mental health strain among researchers, the cracks are showing. 

This post explores what product managers can learn from the academic world’s growing pains and how a decentralized approach could be the key to sustainable scalability.

### Key Insights
- **Hypergrowth without balance can undermine core value.** AI conference publication rates have more than doubled per author in the past decade, leading to a “quantity over quality” problem that product managers will recognize in fast-scaling products.
- **Environmental and logistical strain is real.** Events like NeurIPS now generate more daily CO₂ from travel than their host cities emit.
- **User well-being matters for long-term success.** Over 70% of online conversations about these conferences express negativity, much tied to stress and burnout.
- **Decentralization as a solution.** The proposed *Community-Federated Conference* model decouples review, presentation, and networking into globally coordinated but locally run events—cutting emissions, easing costs, and fostering stronger engagement.

## Why It Matters for Product Managers
The challenges faced by AI conferences mirror what happens when products, platforms, or communities scale without structural change. Relying on incremental fixes (like caps or temporary multi-site setups) addresses symptoms, not causes. By thinking in terms of modular, federated systems, product leaders can create sustainable growth that maintains quality, inclusivity, and user trust.
